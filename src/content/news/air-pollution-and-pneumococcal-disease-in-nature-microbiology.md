---
title: Air pollution and pneumococcal disease in Nature Microbiology
date: 2026-09-14
summary: Which pneumococcal subtypes are circulating changes how air pollution affects the risk of invasive disease in South Africa.
---

We published our paper in *Nature Microbiology*:
[Pneumococcal population structure influences the effects of air pollution on invasive disease risk in South Africa](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41564-026-02458-5).

We followed about 59,000 cases of invasive pneumococcal disease over 19 years in
South Africa's national surveillance programme, together with air pollution and
temperature data. We found that the effect of air pollution depends on which
subtypes of the bacterium are common. Where some subtypes were common, cases
rose within the same week of high pollution; across all cases, risk peaked
about two weeks after exposure. Older adults and young children were the most
vulnerable.

We suggest that cleaner air could lower the risk, and that combining
environmental monitoring with genomic surveillance could help predict outbreaks
and prepare vaccination and hospitals. We did the work with the National
Institute for Communicable Diseases in Johannesburg, the Barcelona
Supercomputing Center and the Wellcome Sanger Institute. Read the
[press release from the Wellcome Sanger
Institute](https://www.sanger.ac.uk/news_item/bacteria-in-airway-shape-air-pollution-health-risk/).
